# Reduced-NFE task evaluation: the same ten-paper audit

Checked 8 November 2026 against the primary papers linked below. These are the same ten representative DLM papers used in `reporting_audit.md`, not a citation-ranked top ten. The relevant question is whether downstream **generated-answer task scores** are measured at fewer diffusion steps or greater tokens-per-forward. Likelihood estimates, Monte Carlo likelihood samples, training steps and generative perplexity are different quantities.

## Findings

| Paper | Task-level evidence at reduced generation compute? | Location and scope |
|---|---|---|
| [LLaDA](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09992) | **Yes: direct step/quality trade-off** | Appendix B.7, Figure 5: vary sampling steps over 32/64/128/256 at a fixed 256-token output length (8/4/2/1 tokens per forward) and report task performance versus throughput on GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval and MBPP, comparing LLaDA 8B Base with Llama3 8B Base. Appendix B.6 separately uses steps equal to answer length for the main generation tables. Thus, a main table at one token/step does not mean that the paper omits reduced-step task evaluation. |
| [Dream 7B](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15487) | **Yes: direct timestep/quality trade-off** | Section 5.5.2, Figure 7: Countdown task performance and speed while varying diffusion timesteps; the text specifically discusses 5–20 steps. A focused planning benchmark, not an exhaustive low-NFE sweep over the entire main suite. |
| [DiffuLLaMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17891) | **Yes: four-task comparison of T=32 versus T=8** | Appendix C.5, Table 11 reports HellaSwag (58.7→47.1), WinoGrande (56.4→52.6), SIQA (43.2→41.9) and PIQA (63.3→57.1). This is a selected-task comparison, not the entire main benchmark suite. Section 4.2 describes likelihood-based multiple-choice scoring, so the exact relationship between this T comparison and a fixed-canvas generated-answer NFE sweep should be qualified. Separately, Figure 3 / Appendix C.1 vary unconditional generation steps using perplexity/diversity; Figure 4 and Appendix C.5 also report unconditional-generation latency. **Correction:** Table 11 was missed in the initial audit; the earlier negative finding is withdrawn. |
| [Scaling up Masked Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18514) | **Related evidence: conditional benchmark scores at different step budgets** | Appendix C.3, Table 13 reports MT-Bench scores at 128/256 steps with CFG and 256/512 without CFG, comparing equal sampling computation across guidance methods. This is task-level evaluation, but changes guidance as well as step budget; it is not a clean fixed-method downstream accuracy sweep. Main GSM8K evaluation uses 256 steps (Appendix B.3). |
| [MDLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07524) | **No generated-answer downstream step-sweep identified** | Generation experiments emphasize generative perplexity, including semi-autoregressive generation in Table 5. GLUE results (Table 4 / Appendix D.6) evaluate fine-tuned representations, not generated answers at varying NFE. The discrete/continuous-T likelihood ablation in Appendix E.3 is not task accuracy under faster decoding. |
| [SEDD](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16834) | **No downstream answer-accuracy step-sweep identified** | Section 5.3 / Figure 1 vary generation steps from 32 to 2,048 for 1,024-token sequences and measure generative perplexity. It also reports conditional-generation MAUVE. Neither those perplexity- and diversity metrics nor its zero-shot likelihood tables establish downstream answer-accuracy sweeps under reduced NFE. |
| [Time-Agnostic Masked Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02908) | **No downstream answer-accuracy step-sweep identified** | Section 6.1, Figure 9 compares 64–1,024-step samplers using generative perplexity and entropy, with explicit parallel unmasking. Its GLUE discussion refers to representation results from earlier work, not a low-NFE generated-answer sweep. |
| [DiffuCoder](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20639) | **Yes: explicit halving of decoding steps** | Figure 1(c), Appendix C.3 / Figure 11 compare HumanEval, HumanEval+, MBPP and MBPP+ scores when diffusion steps are reduced from sequence length to half the sequence length. The paper discusses different training variants and temperatures. “2x” is the paper's decoding-step shorthand; do not automatically interpret it as a measured 2x wall-clock improvement. |
| [LLaDA2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15745) | **Related evidence: benchmark quality versus parallelism** | Section 6.3 / Figure 4 vary denoising threshold and block size on a representative benchmark subset, reporting quality and tokens-per-forward (TPF). Example: threshold .95 gives score 70.15 / 2.55 TPF, while .85 gives 67.90 / 3.31 TPF at block size 32. This is adaptive parallel-decoding analysis rather than a fixed T=L/2 sweep. The prose cross-references Figure 5 inconsistently; the Figure 4 caption identifies the score/TPF analysis. |
| [Mercury](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17298) | **Parallel-system task benchmarks, but no explicit NFE sweep identified** | Tables 1–3 report coding performance for the parallel diffusion deployment; Section 3.2.1 reports separate API throughput measurements. The proprietary sampler's step counts/output-length ratios are not disclosed sufficiently to reconstruct a controlled reduced-NFE sweep. Speed and accuracy columns do not establish that every benchmark was profiled at several NFE settings. |

## Conclusion for the rebuttal

The conditional premise that these papers do not test downstream tasks at reduced NFE is false. At least **LLaDA, Dream and DiffuCoder** provide direct generation-step examples; **DiffuLLaMA also reports a four-task T=32-versus-8 comparison** (Appendix C.5 / Table 11). LLaDA2.0 and the scaling-MDM paper provide related task-level parallelism/compute analyses. We should **not** write that such evaluation is absent or “not usual practice” on the basis of this audit. This purposive sample is not a frequency estimate of the entire field.

A narrower, firmer observation is supported: perplexity- and diversity metrics are an established evaluation approach, and the named task-level examples use selected tasks rather than repeating each paper’s entire benchmark suite at every reduced-NFE budget. A full-suite sweep is not a uniform reporting convention in these examples. “Full suite” means all tasks, not all test questions. This is not a claim that a majority of DLM papers avoid task-level testing, nor a verified universal absence across every paper/version. That context does not replace the reviewer’s requested evidence for BYOD.

## BYOD’s defensible response

Our main task benchmarks use one token transferred per denoising step, but that is not necessarily left-to-right autoregression: predictions use bidirectional context and transfer order can differ. Separately, the existing open-generation experiments generate 128-token canvases at 32, 64 and 128 NFE and report perplexity and Distinct-n. The 32/64-NFE settings therefore test parallel denoising. They do **not** establish task accuracy at those budgets or a wall-clock speedup.

The manuscript's existing BYOD-Gemma perplexities are 14.56 at 32 NFE, 8.27 at 64 and 4.33 at 128, compared with 3.01 for its AR parent. The low-NFE degradation is a real reported limitation, not resolved by calling parallel decoding tested. The contribution is conversion feasibility/compute accessibility, not demonstrated fast generation at matched quality.

Reduced-NFE task accuracy and measured latency remain valuable future work. A compact 1/2/4-token-transfer comparison at fixed output length and shared question IDs would directly address this issue if later authorized, but no such new run is promised or added to the current sample-expansion campaign by this literature check.


## Correction and wording update

On the closer DiffuLLaMA check requested by the author, Appendix C.5 / Table 11 was found to compare four downstream task scores at T=32 and T=8. The initial “no downstream sweep identified” classification was incomplete and is explicitly corrected above. The public response now uses the stronger, supported distinction between generation metrics/selected-task analyses and a complete task-suite sweep at every NFE budget, without asserting field-wide rarity or confusing task coverage with test-set sample counts.


## Comparison with the AR parent at 32 NFE

The rebuttal distinguishes decoding regimes rather than dismissing the AR reference. BYOD-Gemma's 14.56 perplexity is measured at 32 denoising steps on a 128-token canvas; its AR parent's 3.01 is measured under standard sequential AR generation, which has no directly analogous reduced diffusion-NFE setting. The closer within-table reference for the reduced-step diffusion regime is LLaDA at 32 NFE on that same canvas (5.33). BYOD-Gemma at 128 NFE has perplexity 4.33. These historical values come from the working manuscript's `tab:ppl` and should be checked against the submitted version before posting. Equal NFE/canvas does not establish equal compute or latency, and LLaDA still has substantially lower perplexity at 32 NFE. The wording is contextual, not a claim that the AR quality comparison is invalid.
